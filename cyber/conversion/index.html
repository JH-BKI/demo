Convert to Binary

How the converter works

Binary is how computers store numbers — using only 1s and 0s. Each position in an 8-bit binary number has a place value: 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1, reading left to right.

To convert a decimal number to binary, work through the place values from largest to smallest, asking at each one:
“Can this place value fit into my remaining number?”

  • Yes → put a 1 in that position, subtract the place value, and carry the remainder to the next column.
  • No → put a 0 and move on with the same number.

When you've worked through all 8 positions, the 1s and 0s you've written down are the binary equivalent. You can check your answer by adding up the place values of every position marked 1 — the total should equal your starting number.

Try it out: type any number from 0 to 255 into the box and watch each step of the conversion happen. Change the number and see how the working changes. When you're confident you can follow the method, select Begin Quiz to test yourself — you'll be given 10 random numbers to convert on your own.
